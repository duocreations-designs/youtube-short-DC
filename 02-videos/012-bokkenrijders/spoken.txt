The goats could fly, and the court wrote it down.
But nobody in Limburg ever saw one in the sky.
Between seventeen forty three and seventeen ninety four, southern Limburg lived in fear of the Bokkenrijders, the goat riders.
Gangs robbed farms and churches at night, and people said the devil gave them goats that flew.
So the judges went hunting for riders.
Every suspect was tortured until he answered one question: who rides with you?
One prisoner gave three names.
Then those three were tortured, and they gave nine more.
Even Joseph Kerckhoffs, a surgeon from Herzogenrath, was named as their captain, and on May eleventh, seventeen seventy two, he was hanged.
Each confession told the same story: an oath to the devil, a goat at midnight, a ride above the trees.
You see, the judges already knew what a goat rider should say, so the pain made people say it.
The robbers were real, but the flying came from the torture room.
By seventeen ninety four, about twelve hundred people had been accused.
At least four hundred and twenty five of them were executed.
But not one goat was ever found.
So if the pain only stops when you say a name, whose name do you say?
